# House style for concept proposals

How this designer presents a concept, taken from his own proposals. Follow it so the work looks like his.

## What a concept proposal contains
1. Cover: company, "Concept Design (v1) - <client surname>", prepared by, date, reference (e.g. GI/331).
2. **New Garden Layout Proposal** page:
   - **Context**: who commissioned it and what the garden is like now (areas, current surfaces, problems), beside 3-4 site photos.
   - **Brief**: short bullet points of what the client asked for.
3. Coloured concept plan + two aerial 3D views.
4. Elevations (e.g. West / South / East), house and garden in profile against a pale blue sky.
5. Eye-level and aerial views of the key spaces, with a few people for scale.
6. A larger labelled plan of the main area.
7. **Next Steps**: design process, planting plans come later, cost estimates are estimates, optional lighting, and a client approval signature line.

## Level of detail (deliberately loose)
- Zones are named, not specified: PLANTING, PAVING, LAWN, GRAVEL COURTYARD, HEDGE / TRELLIS, COMPOSTING AREA.
- Materials appear as codes or broad names: RBG1 (resin bound gravel), HOGGIN / CRAG / CEDAPATH, PAVING.
- Planting is shown as massed shrubs and trees with no species. Planting plans are a later, paid stage.
- Structures are named by purpose: CARPORT, GARAGE, GREENHOUSE, FIREBOWL, SLATTED SCREEN & HEDGE PLANTERS, EXTENDED ROOF.
- No dimensions, levels or prices on concept drawings.

## Plan look
- Muted sage background around the site, crisp black outlines.
- Lawn mid-green with loose hand-drawn grass strokes; planting beds dark brown with rounded shrub masses; pale paving; warm brown gravel and hoggin; blue water.
- Roofs shown from above in greys.
- Labels: bold black capitals on the plan; grey rounded callout boxes with pointers for structures and features.

## Image look
- SketchUp-style massing with simple trees for 3D views; atmospheric, loose watercolour-style renders for client-facing views.
- Soft English daylight, sage and green palette, white or off-white timber structures, simple people and cars for scale.

## Tone of writing
Plain, warm and professional. Short sentences. Talk about how the client will use and enjoy the spaces, and the problems the design solves (drainage, wind, privacy, access). Avoid jargon and over-promising.
